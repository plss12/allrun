# 013 - The same look on every phone

Date: 2026-11-04

Status: accepted. Replaces the platform's icons, font, glass and colors chosen earlier for the
interface; the widgets and images already drew their own.

## Context

Symmetry and centering are a firm decision of the owner, and the interface relied on pieces that
differ from phone to phone: SF Symbols on iOS and Material on Android, with different shapes and
weights, and off center in their own boxes (saving and exporting needed a lift of 2 pt on iOS);
San Francisco and Roboto, of different widths; Liquid Glass, only on some iPhones; and semantic
colors. A row checked on one iPhone could be off on another phone, and it could only be checked by
eye. Many Android phones are also 360 pt wide, narrower than any iPhone the rows had been checked on.

## Decision

- **Our own icons** (`ui/app-icon.tsx`, generated by `scripts/icons/build-ui-icons.mjs` with
  `npm run icons`): Material Symbols Rounded, outlined, weight 500, drawn by Skia as vectors. Each is
  moved to the middle of its box by its own shape when generated, and a test checks every one with
  CanvasKit, so centering is by construction and not by eye. Those that point sideways (chevrons) go
  by the middle of the triangle around them, as a play button does: by their box, they look off
  toward their open side.
- **A few drawn by us**, by decision of the owner: saving and exporting as a pair (the same tray and
  arrow, in and out), the collages and designs as a frame split in panes that touch (four; two and a
  tall one), and starting over as refreshing in a mirror.
- **Inter for all the interface's text** (`ui/text.tsx`), mapping `fontWeight` to the face of each
  weight: Android ignores weights on custom fonts, and each face is a family of its own. Without
  Android's font padding. Inter's line is centered on its capitals. The screens mount once the faces
  are loaded, under the launch screen, so no text jumps. The activity cards' columns of data, in
  Menlo and Droid Sans Mono until now, use Space Mono, already bundled for the widgets.
- **Our own glass** on the top tabs: `expo-blur` under a translucent gradient and a lit edge. Android
  only blurs what a `BlurTargetView` wraps, so each screen wraps its content in one, and it only blurs
  from Android 12 on: below it, the translucent gradient alone.
- **Fixed colors** (`theme/colors.ts`): iOS's dark values as plain strings on every phone.
- **A lint rule** forbids `Text` from React Native and `expo-symbols`, so nothing goes back by habit.
- **Rows measured for 360 pt** (`editor/editor-layout.ts`, `ui/tabs-layout.ts`, with a test). The tabs
  narrow rather than reach the buttons beside them.
- **Native only what belongs to the system:** alerts, the share sheet, the keyboard, the date picker,
  permissions, the status bar and the back gesture. A user expects those to look like their phone's.

## Rejected

- **Each platform's icons and font, nudged where they look off.** It is what the app had: the nudges
  are per platform and per icon, and only the eye can check them.
- **An icon font.** One native view per screen instead of one per icon, but its vertical position
  depends on each platform's line metrics, which is what this decision wants out.
- **Liquid Glass with a fallback.** It looks the best on the iPhones that have it, and those are the
  only ones: the same screen would look different elsewhere.

## Consequences

- Increase Contrast no longer changes the app's colors, and the interface's text doesn't follow the
  system's font. Dynamic Type still scales it, up to each text's `maxFontSizeMultiplier`.
- Each icon on screen is one Skia canvas. Lists must not repeat them by the hundred; the icons of a
  card are few, and only visible cards are mounted.
- A new icon is a line in the generator and a run of `npm run icons`.
- `borderCurve: 'continuous'` only applies on iOS: rounded corners differ imperceptibly on Android.

## Later

ADR 014 keeps this look but draws the icons with a font instead of a canvas each, and stops blurring the top bar's glass on Android, which keeps its translucent grey, a little darker: on a modest phone, that blur cost on every frame of a scroll.
